01. THE FOOTPRINT

"To match one nuclear reactor, you would need over 3,000,000 solar panels covering thousands of acres."

Nuclear power possesses the highest energy density on the planet. A single uranium pellet, the size of your fingertip, contains as much energy as one ton of coal, 149 gallons of oil, or 17,000 cubic feet of natural gas.

In lifecycle analysis, nuclear emissions are as low as 4g CO2/kWh—lower than solar and comparable to wind. Furthermore, nuclear energy requires 100x less land than renewables to produce the same amount of electricity, preserving our vital natural ecosystems.

02. THE HEARTBEAT

A grid that never sleeps.

Modern civilization runs 24/7, but wind and solar are intermittent—they depend on the weather and the time of day. To bridge the gap when the sun sets and the wind stops, we need a heart that beats without interruption.

Nuclear energy operates with a 92% capacity factor. This means nuclear plants are providing full power to the grid 92% of the time, compared to 35% for wind and 25% for solar. It is the only carbon-free source capable of providing reliable baseload power at scale.
